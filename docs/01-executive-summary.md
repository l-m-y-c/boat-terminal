# 01 – Executive Summary

## The Idea in One Paragraph

Every LMYC boat would have a small, always-on touchscreen terminal in the cabin.  
When a member checks out a boat, they pair their phone with that terminal for the duration of the booking.  
The terminal shows live instrument data (depth, wind, speed, battery, etc.), booking status, and simple boat notes.  
The member’s phone handles maps, cellular connectivity, digital logbook, condition photos, and communication with the club booking system.  
When the booking ends, the pairing is automatically released.

## Why This Approach?

| Approach | Pros | Cons |
|----------|------|------|
| Full commercial MFD (Garmin, Raymarine, etc.) | Polished, integrated | Expensive, closed, overkill for shared fleet |
| Phone-only solution | Cheap, already owned | Phone battery dies, poor sunlight readability, no always-available local display |
| **Hybrid terminal + phone (this proposal)** | Low cost, open, phone does heavy work, terminal always available | Requires some custom development and installation |

## Key Benefits for LMYC

- **Better instrument visibility** in the cabin without expensive new plotters on every boat
- **Clear check-in / check-out** with digital record of who had the boat
- **Easier condition reporting** and engine-hour tracking
- **Low ongoing cost** — hardware is inexpensive and replaceable
- **Member phones already exist** — we leverage them instead of fighting them
- **Open and maintainable** by club technical volunteers

## What It Is Not

- Not a replacement for navigation responsibility or paper charts
- Not a full ECDIS or commercial chartplotter
- Not something that requires constant cellular coverage while sailing
- Not a system that stores sensitive personal data on the boat

## Rough Cost Reality

- Hardware per boat (terminal + cabling + enclosure): **CAD $80–150**
- Development effort: primarily volunteer / club technical members
- Ongoing cost: essentially zero (no subscriptions required for basic operation)

## Recommendation

This is a low-risk, high-value experiment that fits the cooperative spirit of LMYC.  
A single-boat pilot would prove (or disprove) the concept before any wider rollout.
