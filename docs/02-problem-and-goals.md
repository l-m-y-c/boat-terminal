# 02 – Goals & Opportunities

## Building on What Already Works

LMYC already has a clear, web-based online booking system. Members log in, view the calendar, and reserve boats in a straightforward way. The fleet pages provide useful boat details, marina information, maintenance items, and certification requirements.

The boat terminal project is designed to **complement** this existing digital foundation rather than replace it. The aim is to extend the same seamless experience from the web browser onto the boat itself.

## Opportunities to Enhance the On-Board Experience

1. **Bring key boat information into the cabin**  
   Live instrument data (depth, wind, speed, battery, etc.) and current booking status can be visible at a glance without needing to unlock a phone or go on deck.

2. **Create a natural digital bridge between booking and boat**  
   When a member arrives for their reservation, pairing their phone with the boat terminal can confirm the booking, display relevant boat notes, and open a simple channel for logging and issue reporting.

3. **Make maintenance reporting effortless**  
   Members who notice something while on board can capture it immediately (text or photo) and have it flow into the same maintenance tracking the club already uses.

4. **Keep the system open and affordable**  
   By using inexpensive, well-supported hardware and open software, the club can equip the whole fleet without large capital outlay or proprietary lock-in.

5. **Leverage the phones members already carry**  
   The phone remains the rich interface for charts, weather, photos, and club communications. The terminal handles the always-available local display and simple boat-side interactions.

## Project Goals

### Primary Goals

- Provide a clear, always-available local display of key boat data in the cabin
- Create a smooth digital link between the existing booking system and the physical boat
- Make it easy for members to report observations and log hours while on board
- Keep total cost per boat low enough that the club can equip the whole fleet
- Remain maintainable by club volunteers using open tools

### Secondary Goals

- Support future enhancements (simple checklists, visitor information, engine-hour alerts)
- Integrate cleanly with the existing LMYC booking and maintenance systems
- Work offline for core instrument display (cellular only needed for pairing and uploading logs)

### Non-Goals (Explicitly Out of Scope for v1)

- Full chartplotting or route planning on the terminal
- Autopilot control
- Replacing the official logbook or any legal requirements
- Real-time tracking of boats by the club while under way
- High-brightness outdoor / helm displays (cabin focus first)

## Success Criteria for a Pilot

A successful pilot on one boat would demonstrate:

- Reliable display of live NMEA data
- Smooth phone pairing and un-pairing that feels like a natural extension of the booking process
- Members can move from web booking → boat arrival → paired terminal → sail → check-out without friction
- Useful observations or hour logs flow easily into the club’s existing processes
- Hardware is practical and reliable in normal cabin conditions
